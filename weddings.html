Weddings

عروسی

Our celebrations are built on centuries of poetry, hospitality and sacred tradition, and they deserve more than a cookie-cutter approach. We honor the deep soul of your heritage while stripping away the stress, never the meaning.

These are examples; we confirm your family's customs and sequence with you and your officiant.

Sofreh Aghd

سفرهٔ عقد

The spread at the head of the room: mirror, candlesticks, honey, bread, coins, the pomegranate.

The Persian ceremony. We brief your sofreh designer and check the spread against the sofreh key (a one-page guide naming each item on the spread, in the languages listed in your proposal) before the elders are seated.

The key is coordinated with your printer and set out for the guests, so the other family learns each item at a glance.

Nikah

نکاح

The Islamic marriage contract, read before witnesses and both families.

We cue the officiant and the family, hold the elders' seats, and keep the room quiet and steady.

If your family observes the aina mosaf (آینه‌مصحف), as many Afghan families do, it follows the contract as its own moment: the couple's first look in the mirror, the Quran open between them, the elders close. We cue the shawl, the mirror and the reading with your family, and nobody is hurried.

A one-line programme, coordinated with your printer, explains the ritual so every guest can follow.

Afghan weddings · عروسی افغان

Afghan families name their own days, and we use your names: the shirni-khori (شیرینی‌خوری) when the engagement is sealed with sweets, the henna night (khina), the nikah with the aina mosaf, the walima, and the attan if your family dances. We ask which language the elders speak, Dari or Pashto, who leads each moment, and who sits where, and the programme and the parents' pages follow your family's words.

Mehndi & Sangeet

حنابندان

The mehndi is the henna night: music, dance and henna on the bride's hands.

The sangeet is the night both families sing and dance. The baraat is the groom's party arriving with music on the wedding day.

Each one high energy, run to the minute, with the elders' blessing first.

Haldi & Pheras

If your family observes the haldi, the family paints the couple with turmeric before the wedding. The pheras under the mandap, with the pandit: your ceremony, in the sequence agreed with your officiant.

We coordinate timing with your officiant and religious venue. If your family observes the baraat and the milni, we call them in the order of the agreed programme. We hold the elders' seats and keep the mandap quiet and on time.

A one-line programme, coordinated with your printer, explains each ritual so every guest can follow.

Anand Karaj

The Sikh ceremony before the Guru Granth Sahib: the couple walk the four laavan as the hymns are sung. Your ceremony, in the sequence agreed with your family and the granthi.

We coordinate timing with the gurdwara. If your family observes the baraat and the milni, we call them in the order of the agreed programme. We hold the elders' seats and keep the hall quiet and on time.

A one-line programme, coordinated with your printer, explains each ritual and what the gurdwara asks of guests (heads covered, shoes off), so every guest can follow.

Reception & Walima

جشن عروسی · ولیمه

Welcome sign, seating chart and table numbers in the languages listed in your proposal, coordinated with your printer and the venue.

The timeline called with the venue and every vendor. Blessings, entrances and dancing as your family wishes, in the order agreed with your family.

The send-off cued for the elders, and a thank-you card that follows.

Nowruz & Yalda

نوروز · شب یلدا

Spring, and the longest night. If your date falls near Nowruz or Shab-e Yalda, we plan the haft-sin or the Yalda table into the programme with your family and your designer, and we check it against the key on the day.

Two heritages at one table?

We blend cultures across a multi-day celebration without diluting either: each ritual stays as that family keeps it.
