# Build Anar's site around traditions and families

## Bottom line (5 lines)

1. **Build 11 pages at launch.** Center them on the traditions you plan and on the families who host. Don't build them around luxury claims.
2. **Four gaps are open in the Bay Area.** No planner leads with Afghan weddings. Nobody explains the traditions well. No Persian site uses Farsi script. Almost nobody talks to parents.
3. **Copy proven patterns, not sentences.** Use these: your niche in the headline, a named 3-step process, tiers with one-line promises, "starting at" prices, soft buttons and a real inquiry form.
4. **Your nearest rivals win on reviews, not websites.** Those rivals are Aroosi By Lili, Nilou Weddings and Tamtastic Creations. Start collecting Google and WeddingWire reviews from your very first event.
5. **Original Anar copy is ready in section 7.** It covers hero lines, the process, tier names, About prompts and button labels, all in a plain, warm tone.

## Words used in this report

| Word | Plain meaning |
|---|---|
| Hero | The big headline at the top of the home page |
| CTA (call to action) | A button or link that asks the visitor to do something ("Book a call") |
| Nav | The menu at the top of the site |
| Tier | A service package level (for example day-of, partial, full) |
| Landing page | A page built for one topic, often so Google can find it |
| SEO | Getting found on Google |
| Scarcity line | Saying you take a limited number of clients |
| Proof | Anything that shows others trust you: reviews, press, awards |
| Lead magnet | Something free (a guide, a checklist) offered in exchange for an email |
| Inquiry form | The "contact us" form a couple or parent fills out |

## 4. Messaging patterns that work

| Pattern | Who does it well | Why it works | Anar's version |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Niche in the hero** | Lovely Day; Nilou; Marigold | A visitor knows in 3 seconds if they're in the right place | Name Persian, Afghan, South Asian and Bay Area right in the hero |
| **Identity plus place** | Amy Abbott | Feels rooted and local, not generic | Bay Area home base + the cultures you know |
| **Named process** | Calder Clark (3 pillars); Fête Nashville (5 phases); K.I. Weddings (5 steps) | Turns something you can't see into steps people can picture | 3 steps with plain names (section 7) |
| **Tier promise lines** | Aroosi: full planning is "We manage it all." | One short line per tier is easy to scan | Your own tier names and lines (section 7) |
| **Show some price** | Tamtastic, Lovely Day, SJS, Jove Meyer | Screens out bad fits. Directories leak prices anyway | Show "starting at" for each tier |
| **Scarcity** | Bustle (8 a year), Fête (10 a year), Calder Clark | Signals care and demand | Honest version: a few weddings per season, founder on site |
| **Proof** | Banta's awards hub; Nilou's badges; Aroosi's press strip | People trust other people | Start with Google/WeddingWire reviews plus vendor partner names |
| **Family framing** | Karishma Manwani, Momo, BBA, Shaadi Squad | In these cultures the families host | A full page for parents, and family mentions on every page |
| **Ritual vocabulary** | Karishma Manwani (gurdwara, langar, Jaggo); Momo (pandit, imam, granthi) | Shows you've actually done this | Name the real events: Aghd, Nikah, Khina, Mehndi, Baraat |
| **Soft buttons** | Nearly every top site uses talk-style buttons, not "Book now" | Lower pressure means more inquiries | Original labels in section 7 |
| **Reply-time promise** | K.I. Weddings, Tamtastic (24 hours) | Lowers the worry about reaching out | "Reply within one business day" |
| **Bookend** | Sarah Haywood, Monarque | The closing line echoes the hero, so the page feels finished | Repeat your hero idea above the footer |

## 8. Watch-outs seen on peer sites

- **Typos and placeholders hurt trust.** Even award-winning sites had them: "cultual" (Naureen Khan), "Cooke Policy" (Riley Loves Lulu), "Isntagram" (Marquet). Proofread every page.
- **Stale footers look abandoned.** Wedding Shahrzad, Bruce Russell and Designs by Mitra all show old copyright years.
- **Don't self-crown.** Lines like "Top-Rated" or "Most Sought After" with no proof read as weak. Use reviews instead.
- **Don't go minimalist yet.** Image-only home pages work only for famous firms (Easton, Laurie Arons). A new firm needs words.
- **Reviews decide this niche.** Nilou has the weakest site of the Bay Area peers but the strongest reputation, built on 120 reviews ([WeddingWire](https://www.weddingwire.com/biz/wedding-vows-by-nilou-san-francisco/177f2ade0ad124e9.html)). Ask every client for a review within a week.

## Conclusion

Spending more on design will not win the Bay Area's cultural wedding market. The winner will be the planner who explains the traditions, speaks to the families who host, and says "Afghan" out loud. The top general sites show the craft: a niche hero, a named process, honest prices and a qualifying form. The cultural sites show the gap: rituals hidden in photo captions, parents left to the reviews, and no Farsi script.

Anar starts with no press, but that matters less here than it seems. In this niche, trust comes from reviews and from cultural fluency you can show on the page. A new firm can build both in its first season.

**Open questions for the owner:**
- Which languages will you offer?
- Will you show prices?
- What is the real story behind the name?
- Have family elders confirm the Afghan event names and order.
